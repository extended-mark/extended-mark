DECOMPILE FROM AN IR  compiler/xtmd/src/decompile/ir.rs
`xtmd decompile page.ir.json` reads a compact IR (`{"ir": …}`, what `xtmd compile` writes) as well as a
resolved one: from_ir turns it into a resolved IR's file, and the one writer prints it. What the IR
doesn't keep is written anew, each kind of loss said once as a warning (`decompile.lost` in json):
comments, the Markdown's spelling, the order of a call's arguments, what the page doesn't reach,
imports' other names, tokens blocks' names, and a call worked out when compiled (its value is
written). Nothing else may be lost: compiler/xtmd/tests/decompile.rs prints back every page of the
cases, examples and apps from its IR, and holds the IR compiled again to the first, its Markdown too.
What lowering does that has to be undone, so that the same IR comes back:
- widgets in the IR's order, which numbers their handlers; a handler is a lambda in its event
- a hole alone in its paragraph: `declare let ~mN` and an `if` on `sentence(~mN)`
- a call folded in rich text leaves `declare let` locals before the words: their values are put in
- `place` in a sentence, in bold too, is a hole; with a rich view, in a call's block, it is prose
- `hole.TEXT(x)` in inline maths, and `show` around a hole's value
This is how a release's migration reads a page: the old compiler's IR, steps, then decompile.
